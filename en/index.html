Profile

Demography at the core of analytics and public policy

Populations change slowly, but they never stop: fewer children are born, people migrate, grow older and live longer. Those changes define who the students, patients, workers and customers of the future will be. Public policy and business analytics remain incomplete unless they understand and properly project their target population.

David Forero is an economist with a master's in Development Studies and Management and a PhD candidate in Demography at Universidad Nacional de Córdoba. He has analyzed data for the infrastructure, retail, payments, education and health sectors; for public investment projects exceeding USD 730 million over 20-year horizons; and for regional operations across Latin America of more than USD 1 million per month.

His work focuses on the analysis of complex processes through quantitative tools (Bayesian statistics, machine learning, geographic information systems, topological data analysis and AI integrations), to produce population characterizations and projections that are useful, clear and add value.

  • Population characterization and projectionBirth rates, fertility, life expectancy, migration, ageing, socioeconomic characteristics and consumer preferences, for public policy and business intelligence decisions.
  • Quantitative methodsBayesian statistics, machine learning, geographic information systems and topological data analysis.
  • Artificial intelligenceIntegration of tools and scattered data sources to automate analysis, reporting and projections.
  • Large-scale projectsInfrastructure project cash flows above USD 730 million over 20 years and business units above USD 12 million per year.
  • Several industriesRetail, payments, infrastructure, health, education and applied research centers.
  • EditorialOpinion columns on population, economics, technology and development.

Services for businesses and institutions

Demography for business decisions

Markets and technology change constantly. Population, by contrast, follows patterns we can anticipate. Knowing our current customers well and projecting our potential customers is not a bet on the future: it is business intelligence. Tomorrow's customers already exist, even if they have not been born yet. We can identify them, characterize them and anticipate how they will evolve.

01

Population characterization

Who the audiences of interest are, where they live and how: age structure, births, migration, socioeconomic conditions and consumer preferences.

What does the young population look like in the municipalities where the company operates?

02

Population projection

Scenarios by age, sex and territory, with Bayesian models that make uncertainty visible.

How many school-age children will live in this area in 2035?

03

Business and market intelligence

Market sizing, pricing strategy, full-funnel analysis and customer journey mapping, connected to the population ahead.

How large will the target market be over the next ten years?

04

Data products and AI

Dashboards, maps and automated reports fed in real time, integrating QGIS, PostgreSQL, R, Python and AI.

A dashboard that tracks the population of the operating areas.
