Profile

Demography at the center

Populations change slowly, but they never stop: fewer children are born, young people move between regions and society grows older. Those changes define who the students, patients, workers and customers of the next twenty years will be.

David Forero is an economist with a master's in Development Studies and Management and a PhD candidate in Demography at Universidad Nacional de Córdoba. He has analyzed data for higher education, health, infrastructure, fintech and services, across public investment projects, regional operations in Latin America and academic leadership.

Data analytics, business intelligence, large-scale data, web scraping, Bayesian models, topological data analysis and AI integrations with QGIS, PostgreSQL, R and Python. All with one purpose: better estimates and clearer pictures of the population.

Research

Lines of work

Doctoral research in demography and applied public health research, with population data as the starting point.

01

Regional demographic change

The doctoral dissertation follows Colombia's departments from 2005 to 2050: demographic dividend, ageing and economic support ratio. Each territory goes through the transition at its own pace.

02

Population and health

Birth rates and maternal-child service planning, cervical cancer, pesticide exposure and Parkinson's disease, and pediatric asthma; the latter published in 2026 in a Scopus Q3 journal.

03

Health economics

Economic valuation of payment models in breast cancer care, in progress with Hospital Internacional de Colombia.

For organizations

Demography for business decisions

Schools, universities, hospitals, companies and public agencies make long-term decisions. Characterizing and projecting their population makes it possible to anticipate who will be there.

01

Population characterization

Who the audiences of interest are, where they live and how, built from censuses, household surveys and administrative records.

What does the young population look like in the municipalities where the company operates?

02

Population projection

Scenarios by age, sex and territory, with Bayesian models that make uncertainty visible.

How many school-age children will live in this area in 2035?

03

Markets and economic capacity

Size, composition and purchasing power of the markets ahead: children, women, young people and older adults.

Which territories will gain working-age and consuming population?

04

Data products

Dashboards, maps and systems fed in real time, integrating QGIS, PostgreSQL, R, Python and AI.

A dashboard that tracks the population of the operating areas.
